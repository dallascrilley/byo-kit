## Check the evidence

For each consequential claim, record the exact source revision, test/result or reproducible command. Distinguish observations, illustrative inputs, inferences and opinions. Check prior art before claiming novelty. A benchmark needs its baseline and conditions. Do not turn development speed into production reliability, a synthetic example into a customer story, or interest into adoption. Keep secrets, customer data, job applications and retrieved writing samples out of public material.

Use the author's writing style. When available, retrieve appropriate samples through the authorized style connector at drafting time. Do not invent a style match, save a sample cache, or copy old facts. Use supplied examples if retrieval is unavailable. Apply the current prose guidance while writing and editing.

## Write the short article

Aim for 400 to 800 words; the hard ceiling is 999 including the title and link text. One main insight and one useful example are enough. Explain the decision, what happened, the tradeoff and what another builder could reuse. Link to the published repository and usable demo when there is one. Start with the lesson rather than a résumé, launch announcement or build diary. Use a straightforward title that the evidence earns. Make ownership clear without sales copy or calls for votes/stars.

Draft docs/CASE_STUDY.md. After the actual project release, run `npm run blog:check`. It checks length, placeholders and links against the release receipt. It cannot judge the insight, factual truth, writing style or community fit. Read and edit the whole article before publication. ## Distribution is a separate step

Choose one relevant community by its actual subject and recent rules. Check current self-promotion and AI-content rules. Prepare the precise link and a useful native discussion opener; disclose authorship and do not make it sound like an independent discovery. Check the current Hacker News guidelines directly. At the 2026-10-27 check, they prohibit generated or AI-edited on-site text and automated posting. Keep HN submissions and comments manual and human-written. A blog is a regular submission; Show HN is for a substantial project people can try and discuss, not quickly generated one-offs. These notes do not establish eligibility for any particular project. See https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html and https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html; recheck before use. Do not promise upvotes, coordinate votes, mass cross-post, or manufacture engagement.

Record the live article URL after publication. Do not treat the local receipt as editorial certification. Community posts, unrelated website changes and external outreach remain separate actions under their existing authority.

Include drafting/editing time in the measured project workflow. A reusable outline saves time; a rushed unsupported take does not meet the goal. Article publication is a separate result from project publication.
